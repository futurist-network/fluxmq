Root cause. The spikes come from the environment, not from v0.8 code:

  • Both images show them. The v0.7 image shows them as often and as large as v0.8 when the runs alternate (A/B rows), so they are not caused by v0.8 changes.
  • Retention is not involved. The suspected 50 ms retention passes delete nothing in a latency run: bench channels are Stream mode and a 30 s latency run writes at most ~2 GiB, below the 4 GiB cap (sampled peak 1.9 GiB at 50k). A pass with nothing due only counts segments under read locks.
  • Fetch-ahead is not involved. The 1-node rows have no replication.
  • The storage engine's fsyncs are healthy inside the spiky windows. In the sampler-on 1-node runs the fsync mean was 0.6–1 ms and p99 ≤ 2.5–10 ms, while the publish p99 was 110–223 ms.
  • Outside load raises them. Anything that adds work on the host raises the tails: docker exec every 2 s (sampler on vs off), and the macOS storage indexer running at 50–90 % CPU all session. The harness timestamps from the scheduled send time, so any pause of the client, broker or VM vCPU counts.

Systems that fsync before the ack are hit harder (Kafka-fsync's p99 at 50k rose from 57 to 104 ms). Kafka's default acks from the page cache and was barely affected on 1 node.

The 3-node p99 at 50k msg/s comes from a different, structural cause, the same in v0.7 and v0.8: three brokers fsync the same data on one SSD. The p99 follows the fsync mean in the three extra rounds: fsync mean 2.0 ms gives 136 ms, 3.2 ms gives 142, 5.3–5.6 ms gives 203–366. No code change was made: nothing in the v0.8 code is implicated, and the 3-node tail needs the local-commit work listed in docs/design/v0.8-replication.md §5.
